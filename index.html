Customers review a dealership on many different websites. Keeping up means checking each one, writing a reply in the right tone, and doing it before an unhappy customer feels ignored.

Review Responder brings those reviews into one place. When a new review arrives, it drafts a reply in your dealership's voice and tells the right people. The A3 team checks the reply before it goes anywhere. Your dealership gets a portal and a monthly report showing how its reputation is doing.

AI writes the first draft. A person decides what is published.

What it does for you

  • One view of your reviews from seven platforms, so nothing depends on someone remembering to check each site.
  • A reply drafted for each new review, based on what that customer actually wrote.
  • A prompt email to your dealership when a new review arrives, with a link to that review.
  • Follow-up on the reviews that matter most. A reply to a 1 or 2 star review that is still waiting after 48 hours goes to the A3 leads.
  • Monthly reports that A3 checks before you see them.

A good reply sounds like your dealership and shows that someone read the review.

  • It follows your voice. The draft draws on replies your dealership has already posted or approved: how you greet people, how long you write, how you sign off.
  • It only mentions what the customer said. It doesn't add details or make up what happened.
  • Its length suits the review. A short five star review gets a short thank-you. A complaint gets a reply that addresses the problem raised.

Example: praise

Sarah M. ★★★★★

Second car I've bought here. Ask for Danny, no pressure at all.

Thank you, Sarah, and thank you for coming back to us. I'll pass this straight on to Danny. Enjoy the new car.

Example: complaint

Tom R. ★

Waited three hours for an oil change and nobody told me why.

Tom, we're sorry about the long wait and that nobody kept you informed. We'd like to hear more and put this right. Please call our service team directly.

These two examples are made up to show the style. They aren't real customers or real replies.

1 and 2 star reviews

On a negative review, the draft is a starting point. It acknowledges the problem and offers to help, but it can't know what happened at your dealership. A good reply adds a specific detail and a real contact, doesn't argue in public, doesn't name an employee, and goes out quickly.

Nothing is published without a person's decision. This is the most important rule in the tool.

Who approves

The A3 team checks every draft first. Dealership owners don't need to approve anything or sign in for a reply to be handled. They see the review and can follow up with the customer themselves.

How a draft can't get lost

  • A daily reminder goes to the approver each morning while replies have been waiting too long.
  • 1 and 2 star reviews are escalated. After 48 hours without approval, the A3 leads are emailed each day until they're cleared.
  • One decision per reply. If two people click Approve, only the first counts.

When your team replies first

If your dealership has already answered a review on the listing, the tool stops offering its draft. A review doesn't end up with two replies from you. This check depends on the platform showing replies, and some don't, so tell A3 if you have answered somewhere the tool can't see.

Each month gets a report on what customers said and how the dealership responded. It's drafted automatically after the month ends.

  • The figures come from the reviews the tool holds, including responses and trends.
  • A written summary and two or three recommendations are drafted by AI from that month's figures and comments.
  • Staff shout-outs list employees customers praised by name, with a line from a review.
  • A reputation score shows its formula openly, so you can see how it was reached.
  • Outside reference figures are quoted with their source and date, and aren't presented as your own results.
An A3 team member checks each report and can rewrite its text. You only see it once it's published.

Individual reviews tell you about one customer. These features look across many.

Sentiment

The Sentiment view sums up what reviews say, including what positive reviews praise and how ratings are spread. Because platforms publish different things, each figure says what it covers. Complaint themes are matched from review wording, so treat them as a guide.

Recurring complaint alerts

When the same problem shows up in several 1 and 2 star reviews within a month, such as long waits in service or finance not calling back, the A3 team gets an alert with those reviews, so it doesn't have to wait for the monthly report.

Nearby competitors

The Competitors view sets your Google reviews beside those of nearby dealerships of the same brand, using public Google data read once a week. Each rival figure shows when it was read.

What A3 will ask you for

  • Your dealership's name and the review platforms you're listed on
  • Access to your Google Business Profile, so that listing can be read
  • The email addresses that should get "New review" emails and portal logins

How figures stay accurate

  • One counting rule everywhere. Each customer counts once, and only reviews still listed count. The portal, reports and emails all use the same rule.
  • Removed reviews leave your figures. If Google takes a review down, it stops counting, and it comes back automatically if Google shows it again.
  • Your Google total is checked against Google's own. The rating and total refresh on each read.
  • Estimates are labelled. Where something is estimated, such as complaint themes, the tool says so.

How your account is protected

  • Two-step sign-in. A password alone doesn't open an account. It also takes an emailed code or a passkey.
  • Your dealership only. The server checks each request, so a login can only reach the dealerships linked to it.
  • Encrypted Google connection. The key for reading your Google listing is stored encrypted.

How your data is protected

  • Nightly backups. The review data is backed up every night, and each backup is test-restored to prove it works.
  • Monitoring. A watchdog checks the system every five minutes, tries to restart it if something has stopped, and alerts the A3 team.

Can anything be posted without approval?

No. A reply is only published after a person has approved it. If nobody approves it, nothing is posted.

Do I have to approve replies myself?

No. The A3 team checks and approves drafts. You get the "New review" email and can use your portal whenever you want.

What if I already replied to a review?

Where the platform shows replies, the tool sees yours and stops offering its draft. On platforms that don't show them, tell A3 so a second reply isn't posted.

Why do some reviews have no star rating?

Some platforms don't publish a rating for each review. The tool leaves it blank and doesn't guess.

Why do some reviews have no text?

Customers can leave a rating without a comment. These reviews still count toward your rating.

Will I get emails about old reviews?

No. Your history is collected so your figures are complete, but "New review" emails and drafts are only for recent reviews.

Can I change who gets the emails?

Yes. Send your A3 team the addresses you want to use.

Can other people at my dealership see the portal?

Yes. Ask A3 to add a login for each person. Every login sees only the dealerships linked to it.
